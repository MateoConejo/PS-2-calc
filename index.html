1. Analysis of the rate at which variables change

To evaluate how energy consumption changes with outdoor temperature, we must take the derivative of our piecewise function that happens to be our model.

Piecewise Function: $$E(T) = \begin{cases} 6.6 & \text{when } T \le 24^\circ\text{C} \\ 6.6 + 1.2(T - 24) & \text{when } T > 24^\circ\text{C} \end{cases}$$
First Derivative with respect to $T$: $$\frac{dE}{dT} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{when } T \le 24^\circ\text{C} \\ 1.2 & \text{when } T > 24^\circ\text{C} \end{cases}$$

Based on the analysis we can get some conclusions based on the temperature:

1. When temperature is less or equal to $24^\circ\text{C}$ energy consumption will not change and therefore temperature change (below our domain) will not make an impact on the consumption of the model.

2. When temperature is above $24^\circ\text{C}$ daily energy consumption increases at a rate of 1.2 kWh per additional degree of temperature.

2. Energy consumption optimization

To optimize the model, it is required to find the critical points where $\frac{dE}{dT} = 0$ or where the derivative is undefined. It is important to make clear that the critical points must be inside of the domain which in this case is $[19^\circ\text{C}, 33^\circ\text{C}]$.

A. Global minimum consumption

The minimum value occurs at any temperature $T \le 24^\circ\text{C}$. For this part of the piecewise function, $E(T)$ is equal to 6.6. And because there is no $T$ in which we can place values for $T \le 24^\circ\text{C}$, the local minimum will always be 6.6 kWh regardless of temperature $T \le 24^\circ\text{C}$.

B. Global maximum consumption

The maximum value occurs at the extreme of the domain which is the highest temperature possible ($T = 33^\circ\text{C}$):

$E_{\max} = E(T) = 6.6 + 1.2(T - 24)$

$E_{\max} = E(33) = 6.6 + 1.2(33 - 24)$

$E_{\max} = 17.4\text{ kWh/day}$

3. Exploration of insulating materials and passive ventilation

To lower the system’s dependence on outdoor temperature, it is suggested that the following modifications are implemented:

Modification 1

Adding thermal insulation by layers in the walls and roof made of expanded polystyrene will reduce the thermal transmittance coefficient, lowering the slope from 1.2 to approximately 0.7 kWh/°C.

Modification 2

Installing double glazed windows will prevent heat to be trapped in such a small space, shifting the cooling threshold from 24°C to 26°C.
